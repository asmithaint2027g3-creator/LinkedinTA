Telling a regulator or a judge 'the neural network chose this output' is a fast-track to multi-million dollar fines and injunctions.

Under the EU AI Act, US FTC enforcement, and global financial regulations, high-impact automated decisions must be accompanied by mathematically explainable rationales.

1. The End of the Black Box: Regulators no longer tolerate uninterpretable deep learning models in credit scoring, claims adjudication, or healthcare triage.

2. Feature Attribution Methods: Integrating SHAP (Shapley Additive exPlanations) and LIME directly into inference serving to quantify exact decision drivers.

3. Counterfactual Explanations: Generating actionable explanations: 'If variable X had been 12% lower, the approved outcome would have been Y.'

4. Immutable Audit Trails: Recording input data, model version, temperature, prompt template, and attribution values in tamper-evident logs.

Explainability is no longer an academic research topic. It is an operational prerequisite for running AI in regulated industries.
